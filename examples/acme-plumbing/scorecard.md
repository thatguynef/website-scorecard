## Revenue opportunities to investigate (owner data required)

- **Speed-to-lead** — A fast first response is often the difference between a booked job and a competitor's job. The public site cannot prove response time.
- **After-hours and emergency handling** — Emergency demand does not wait for office hours. A visible emergency path is not proof that a human answers it.
- **Lead quality and routing** — A form can collect data and still fail to route the right job to the right person.
- **Booking capacity and confirmation** — An online booking can create a bad customer experience if the calendar is wrong or the office cannot absorb the slot.
- **Estimate pipeline** — Open estimates are often the largest recoverable revenue pool. The website cannot show how many exist.
- **Estimate close rate by source and size** — The same website can produce very different revenue depending on close rate, job size, and lead source.
- **Estimate follow-up** — Unclosed estimates are a high-value opportunity that a public website audit cannot observe.
- **Customer history and job completion** — Repeat and referral revenue depends on knowing who bought, what was installed, and when the next service is due.
- **Review requests** — A strong review profile is public, but the review-request process is not.
- **Referral operations** — Referrals are often the lowest-cost lead source, but they are rarely visible on the website.
- **Reactivation and repeat business** — Old leads, unsold estimates, and inactive customers can produce revenue without new ad spend.
- **Marketing attribution and channel profitability** — Tags can show that traffic arrived; only business data can show which channel produces closed revenue.
