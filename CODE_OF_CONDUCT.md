# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want this project to be a useful, respectful place for contractors, marketers, developers, and researchers to improve a diagnostic tool. We commit to making participation harassment-free regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, experience level, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Expected behavior

- Be direct about the work and kind about the person.
- Critique evidence and methodology, not people.
- Assume good faith, but ask for evidence when a claim matters.
- Respect privacy. Do not publish a client's data, a customer's PII, or a live credential.
- Accept that a finding may be wrong and correct it.
- Keep contractor-facing language plain and respectful. The business owner is the audience, not the punchline.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, or discriminatory comments.
- Publishing private information without consent.
- Publishing a real client's audit, screenshots, credentials, or customer data.
- Personal attacks, trolling, or sustained disruption.
- Sexualized language or imagery in project spaces.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern.

## Scope

This applies in repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and any project space. It also applies when representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove comments, close issues, reject contributions, or block participants who violate this code. Enforcement decisions should be proportionate and explained when possible, without disclosing private reports.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, contact a maintainer privately through their GitHub profile. Do not open a public issue that contains personal or sensitive details. Reports will be handled as confidentially as practical.

## Attribution

This is a lightweight code of conduct inspired by the Contributor Covenant. It is intentionally short so it can be read and applied.
