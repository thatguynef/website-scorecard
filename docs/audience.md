# Who This Is For

**Website Scorecard is built for local home-service businesses** — the trades people call when something at home is broken, dirty, unsafe, or overdue — and for the marketers, agencies, and analysts who serve them.

If your customers find you in a local search, call or fill out a form, and book a job, this framework was written for you. Everything in these docs assumes that context.

## The businesses

You are a fit if most of these are true:

| Question | Fit if... |
|---|---|
| What do you sell? | A local, scheduled service: repair, installation, maintenance, inspection, or restoration |
| Where do you sell it? | A city, county, metro, or defined service area — not nationwide e-commerce |
| How do jobs start? | Phone calls, forms, bookings, estimate requests, or messages |
| Who decides? | A homeowner or property manager choosing a local provider |
| Why a website? | It is supposed to turn visitors into booked jobs, not just collect page views |

## Trades

Plumbing · HVAC · Roofing · Electrical · Pest control · Garage door · Landscaping · Restoration · Cleaning · Moving · Septic · Pool service · Fencing · Painting · Similar local trades

The framework is trade-agnostic. Trade and market are audit inputs, not hardcoded assumptions.

## The people

- **Owners and operators** of small and mid-sized home-service companies who want to know where the website is losing ready-to-buy jobs.
- **In-house marketers** who need a repeatable, evidence-based second opinion instead of another SEO checklist.
- **Local agencies and consultants** who diagnose before they prescribe.
- **Analysts and auditors** who need a documented method, machine-readable output, and a verification gate.

## Not a fit

> [!WARNING]
> Website Scorecard is a diagnostic framework, not a growth guarantee. If you need one of the following, this is the wrong tool.

- **SaaS, e-commerce, media, or content businesses** with no local service area or booked jobs.
- **National brands** with mature analytics, call-tracking, and experimentation teams.
- **Franchises or multi-location groups** that cannot change their own website or follow-up process.
- **Anyone who wants a guaranteed revenue lift**, automated fixes, or a replacement for CRM, call-tracking, booking, and financial data.
- **Anyone who wants a vanity score** without evidence. The output separates what the site proves from what only the owner can answer.

## Is my business a fit?

Answer these five questions:

1. Do you sell a service that happens at the customer's location or a local shop?
2. Do you serve a defined city, county, or metro area?
3. Do leads arrive by phone, form, booking, or estimate request?
4. Can you change the website, or advise someone who can?
5. Are you willing to answer owner-data questions about follow-up, response time, and close rates?

**Five yeses:** you are the intended audience. **Three or four:** the website score is still useful, but some owner-data questions may not apply. **Two or fewer:** the framework will still run, but treat the score as background context rather than a diagnosis.

## What the score does and does not mean

| It does | It does not |
|---|---|
| Score only what the public site can prove | See the CRM, phone system, booking capacity, or estimate pipeline |
| Separate **VERIFIED**, **STRONG SIGNAL**, **OWNER DATA REQUIRED**, and **NOT APPLICABLE** | Claim that "not observed" means "not happening" |
| Produce a one-page report, a full audit, and machine-readable output | Guarantee revenue lift or replace a growth strategy |
| Ask the owner the questions the site cannot answer | Pretend a public audit is a full business diagnosis |
